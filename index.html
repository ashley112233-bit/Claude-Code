The missing information

Booking platforms already capture a great deal about a match. They capture very little about the court itself.

Booking data already shows

  • Venue and court
  • Booking time
  • Match format
  • Player level
  • Match completion and result

Court intelligence adds

  • Perceived surface speed
  • Natural and artificial-light interference
  • Roof interference
  • Wind and environmental effects
  • How conditions vary by broad time period

Booking data shows what happened. Court intelligence adds how the court played.

Why players might take part

A short, optional question could give players genuinely useful information, without asking them to write reviews:

  • Learn how an unfamiliar court may play.
  • Compare morning and evening conditions.
  • Understand whether a surface is slow, medium or fast.
  • Make a more informed private booking where court choice exists.

What the pilot is designed to prove

Five clear, testable questions — nothing more is being assumed, and nothing less will be examined.

  • Do enough players answer a short post-match question?
  • Which questions produce consistent, useful information?
  • Does the interaction fit naturally into the existing player journey?
  • Do booking platforms and venues find the aggregated output useful?
  • Can privacy, data ownership and operational responsibilities be agreed satisfactorily?

The pilot is an evidence-gathering exercise, not a commitment to a national rollout.

Honest objections

The questions a senior decision-maker would ask first — answered directly.

Why would a booking platform participate?

To test a potentially differentiating court-data feature across five venues before committing to a full internal build. Padel Court Check would provide the question methodology, aggregation approach and pilot analysis; the partner would receive the evidence needed to decide whether the feature merits further investment.

Will venues see individual criticism?

The concept uses structured answers and aggregated reporting. Individual written reviews are outside the proposed pilot.

What if players do not respond?

The pilot should stop or change. Player participation is a go-or-no-go test.

Could this reduce court bookings?

That risk must be tested. The service is intended to describe court characteristics and time-dependent conditions, not label courts as good or bad.

Who owns the resulting data?

Ownership, permitted use, retention and access must be agreed contractually before any live data is collected.

Why not build this internally?

A booking platform could choose to build its own feature. The purpose of the pilot is to test whether Padel Court Check's specialist question methodology, privacy-preserving aggregation and court-condition focus can provide a faster and more focused route to evidence before that investment decision is made.
