The missing information

Booking platforms already capture a great deal about a match. They capture very little about the court itself.

Booking data already shows

  • Venue and court
  • Booking time
  • Match format
  • Player level
  • Match completion and result

Court intelligence could add

  • Perceived surface speed
  • Natural and artificial-light interference
  • Roof interference
  • Wind and environmental effects
  • How conditions vary by broad time period

Booking data shows what happened. Court intelligence adds how the court played.

What the booking platform could gain

A successful integration could, over time and subject to partner agreement, help a booking platform do the following. None of this has yet been proven.

Differentiate the platform

Add a court-condition feature that competitors may not offer.

Add court-level data

Build an aggregated picture of how individual courts play at different times, for use where court selection is available.

Support participating venues

Give venues structured, aggregated evidence about court conditions rather than isolated complaints.

Test without a major build

Explore the concept through a small, bounded pilot rather than an internal engineering commitment.

A privacy-preserving, aggregated court intelligence concept

The proposed flow is designed to keep individual answers separate from public identity, and to report on courts in aggregate rather than on individual players or matches.

Aggregated court information

  • No public written reviews.
  • No reviewer names shown.
  • Individual answers would not be exposed to venues.
  • No exact match details published.
  • Public information, if later agreed, would be aggregated rather than attributed to one match or player.
  • Technical roles, data ownership, retention and lawful processing would all need agreement before any pilot began.

This describes the intended approach, not a guarantee of complete or absolute anonymity. The precise privacy and data-sharing structure would be agreed jointly with the booking partner before a pilot.

Why players might use it

This is the least-tested part of the concept, and we say so plainly below. If it were useful to players, it might help them:

  • Learn how an unfamiliar court may play.
  • Compare morning and evening conditions.
  • Understand whether a surface is slow, medium or fast.
  • Make a more informed private booking where court choice exists.
Player demand is the first question the pilot must test. We are not assuming that players will complete the interaction.

Proposed limited pilot

A deliberately small, bounded pilot, designed to be reviewed jointly and stopped or changed at any point.

  • 5 participating venues
  • 8 weeks
  • 1 UK region
  • A small rotating question set
  • No public written reviews
  • Aggregated reporting only
  • Joint review of player participation and venue response
  • No commitment to a national rollout

Outcome checklist

  • Do players respond?
  • Which questions generate useful information?
  • Do venues value the aggregate?
  • Does the interaction fit naturally after a match?
  • Can privacy and data roles be agreed satisfactorily?

Honest objections

The questions a senior decision-maker would ask first — answered directly.

Why would a booking platform participate?

To test whether specialist court-condition data adds useful platform and venue information. The pilot is designed to answer this rather than assume it.

Will venues see individual criticism?

The concept uses structured answers and aggregated reporting. Individual written reviews are outside the proposed pilot.

What if players do not respond?

The pilot should stop or change. Player participation is a go-or-no-go test.

Could this reduce court bookings?

That risk must be tested. The service is intended to describe court characteristics and time-dependent conditions, not label courts as good or bad.

Who owns the resulting data?

Ownership, permitted use, retention and access must be agreed contractually before any live data is collected.

Why not build this internally?

The proposed value of Padel Court Check is specialist methodology, cross-venue analysis and operating focus. A pilot would test whether that is more useful than an internal feature.

Help us test the question before anyone builds the answer.
